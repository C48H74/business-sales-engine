# Cold email: good versus bad

**Synthetic examples.** The company, announcement and service below are fictional; in real use verify the source/date and permission/channel context. These demonstrate editing choices, not proven response rates.

## Bad

Subject: Transform your operations with revolutionary AI!

"Hope you're well. We're the leading provider of cutting-edge engineering solutions. We know your team is struggling with delays. Our AI guarantees 40% savings. I have two exclusive slots left today. Can you book thirty minutes?"

Problems: fake knowledge of pain, unsupported leadership/ROI, irrelevant category pitch, invented scarcity and a meeting request before relevance. Fluent personalization does not repair these.

## Better

Subject: Model review for the new project team

"Your fictional project announcement mentions expanding the design team. If handoff consistency is part of that work, we can review a bounded sample of the model and return an issue register with exclusions and priorities. We do not certify the design. Would a one-page sample of the review format be useful? If this is outside your priorities, no response is needed."

Why better: observable context, tentative relevance, specific deliverable, honest limit and small optional action. Include actual sender identity and the opt-out mechanism appropriate to the campaign. No customer result is asserted.

## Follow-up with new value

"One check in that review is whether issue owners and acceptance criteria are visible at handoff. Here is the sample format you requested. Does that match the kind of review you need, or is another part of the workflow more relevant?"

Only say "requested" if it was requested. Stop after a refusal. Compare relevant replies and qualified opportunities, not open rates. See [outbound](../references/outbound.md).
