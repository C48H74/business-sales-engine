# Discovery: recent behavior and the gap

Use [call guide](../templates/discovery-call-guide.md) and [transcript](../examples/discovery-call-transcript.md). SPIN supplies question types; The Mom Test motivates learning from behavior instead of flattering predictions. These are lenses, not an interrogation sequence. [S1/S2/S3](sources.md)

Prepare public facts and a hypothesis. Agree purpose/time and whether a solution discussion helps. Separate research (learn without selling) from sales discovery (mutual fit). Follow applicable recording permission requirements.

- **Situation:** "Walk me through the last time." Capture workflow, people, frequency and tools; avoid re-asking known facts.
- **Problem:** "Where did it break down? What did you try?" Investigate friction and alternatives, including manual work.
- **Implication:** "Who was affected? How was the effect measured?" Verify frequency/cost without amplifying fear.
- **Need-payoff:** "What would a useful improvement allow?" Learn success measures and needed buyer changes.

Gap map: current state → root cause/hypotheses → impact → target state → blockers. Distinguish symptom (late reports), mechanism (approval queue), desired result (reliable cutoff) and solution assumption (new software). The product may not fix the mechanism.

Bad: "Would you pay for an AI assistant that saves time?" Better: "What did you buy or build after the last delay? Who approved it?" Ask for evidence, not secret information or commitments beyond the contact's authority.

Reflect a short diagnosis and invite correction. Ask about criteria, funding, affected roles and timing proportionately. End with an agreed action or explicit pause/no-fit. Capture facts, supported quotes, unknowns and verification owners. A title or friendly conversation does not establish purchasing authority.
