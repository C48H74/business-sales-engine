# Committee and internal consensus

Use [committee map](../templates/buying-committee-map.md) and [mutual action plan](../templates/mutual-action-plan.md). Multiple contacts do not prove consensus; one person may hold several roles. Original synthesis linked to MEDDPICC [S4](sources.md).

Map problem owner, economic buyer, users, technical/security reviewers, procurement/legal, implementation owner and champion. Record each role's outcome, mandatory criteria, concerns, influence, stance, evidence and next action. Confirm authority; avoid personality/demographic guesses.

Ask the initial contact: "Who will use this, who could block it and who approves the change? Would a joint review help, or is there a preferred route?" Explain why involvement helps. Do not bypass the contact or mass-message colleagues to fabricate coverage.

Align a shared problem and success criteria, then expose legitimate differences: finance needs cash timing, users need workflow, IT needs portability, procurement needs terms. Give the champion a one-page case with benefits, rollout effort, tradeoffs and unresolved risk.

A champion demonstrates internal advocacy: organizes colleagues, corrects the case or helps resolve criteria. A friendly senior contact is insufficient evidence. Where direct executive access is unavailable, follow the approved route and mark uncertainty.

Red flags: one supporter claims to represent everyone; criteria conflict; nobody owns rollout; legal/security lead time is omitted. Treat these as alignment/feasibility tasks, not pressure to sign. Exit when owners agree or assign the unresolved issues. Collect only information needed for the decision.
