# Negotiate value, scope and terms

Use [planner](../templates/negotiation-planner.md) and [business case](../templates/business-case-onepager.md). Black Swan's calibrated questions and *Never Split the Difference* inform conversation design [S6](sources.md). This is an adapted process, not mandatory use of a bargaining script.

Prepare genuine full price, cost/capacity exposure, target terms, reservation terms, authority and BATNA (best alternative to agreement). Understand buyer alternatives without inventing them. An anchor is the actual defensible offer, not a fictional list price.

State price, scope and payment, then pause. Diagnose resistance: affordability, comparison, return uncertainty, timing or procurement policy. Use tentative reflections and practical what/how questions.

| Need | Conditional exchange | Verify |
|---|---|---|
| Lower cost | Less scope/coverage for lower price | Outcome remains achievable |
| Earlier start | Real capacity if dependencies arrive by a real date | Delivery approval |
| Cash timing | Installments linked to milestones | Seller can fund delivery |
| Discount | Valuable prepayment or reduced support obligation | Contribution and authority |

"We can do X if you can do Y." Record concession cost and received value. Never trade for a positive testimonial; publication and honest evidence remain optional. Splitting the difference can be defensible, but is not the default.

Ackerman-style decreasing concession increments can help plan a bounded range. Do not apply percentages mechanically: they may produce misleading anchors or exceed price authority. Transparent scope choices often serve fixed-price services better.

Confirm scope, price, dependencies, approvals and unresolved terms in writing. Silence is not acceptance. Pause/decline beyond contribution floors, authority or delivery ability. See [negotiation example](../examples/negotiation-case.md).
