# Diagnose objections before scripting

Use [library](../objections/library.md) for fifteen examples, [JOLT](jolt.md) for supported indecision and [negotiation](negotiation.md) for terms. These are original conversation patterns, not automatic rebuttals.

1. Acknowledge the concern without agreeing to an unsupported premise.
2. Ask one question to distinguish value, affordability, fit, risk, timing, authority and refusal.
3. Reflect the buyer's answer and confirm the diagnosis.
4. Supply relevant evidence, a scoped option or a practical decision aid.
5. Agree a verification/action or accept a pause/no.

"Too expensive" can mean insufficient return, a cheaper comparable alternative, unavailable funds or simply no interest. Do not respond to all four with a cost-of-inaction calculation. "Already have a supplier" may mean satisfactory coverage: replacing it might not help.

Example: "Compared with what option, or is the issue this period's budget?" If comparison: compare equivalent scope/terms honestly. If funding: explore a viable smaller scope or defer; do not turn a payment problem into pressure. If uncertainty: verify the assumptions rather than offering an immediate discount.

Log the supported quote, diagnosis, evidence supplied, buyer response and owner/date. If the buyer declines further discussion, stop. Patterns across **comparable** deals can inform changes to targeting, proof or delivery. Do not use objection counts from a small convenience sample as market prevalence.

No threats of regulatory noncompliance, fabricated competitor weaknesses, guilt, shame or scarcity invented to close a hesitant deal. Public industry examples must be relevant and attributed, not implied to be your own customer successes.
