# Qualification for complex deals

Use [scorecard](../templates/meddpicc-scorecard.md) and [deal review](../checklists/deal-review.md). MEDDPICC covers value, stakeholders and process [S4](sources.md). It is not a mathematical win probability. Scoring below is a local convention.

| Element | Evidence | Question |
|---|---|---|
| Metrics | Baseline, impact, success measure | How will you know it worked? |
| Economic buyer | Authority to fund the decision | Who approves spending and what do they require? |
| Decision criteria | Business, technical, risk requirements | Which are mandatory and who owns them? |
| Decision process | Evaluations, people, approval sequence | What happens from evaluation to approval? |
| Paper process | Procurement, security, legal, signature/payment | What follows selection before work starts? |
| Implicate/identify pain | Problem, consequences, reason to change | What happens if the current approach stays? |
| Champion | Influence and actual internal advocacy | Who can organize the decision and has done so? |
| Competition | Vendor, build, priorities, status quo | What competes for these resources? |

Record each as unknown, buyer-reported, documented or validated by its accountable owner (0/1/2/3). This measures evidence completeness; no universal pass score or conversion odds. Report contradictions and critical gaps separately. A high total cannot offset an unmet mandatory requirement.

For small purchases use problem, fit, decision owner, feasibility and next action. BANT can inform conversation; lack of an existing budget is not automatic rejection when a credible case may support funding.

Advance on a confirmed problem and viable evaluation path. Hold for unresolved ownership, funding or rollout. Nurture with permission and a dated trigger. Stop for impossible fit, no viable consented action or buyer refusal. Disqualification is useful.
